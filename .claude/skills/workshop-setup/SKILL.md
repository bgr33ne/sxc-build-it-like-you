The person is checking, before a workshop, that you can write in this folder and publish a page. Keep it quick and playful. Keep it to the check: this page is a one-off, separate from their site, and CLAUDE.md's guidance is for the site. The check adds one folder, `setup-check/`, and its published page, and puts their name into CLAUDE.md.

Start by asking exactly this:

> Welcome! Let's make sure you're set up. But first, what's your first name?

After they reply, ask exactly this:

> What's one of the first things you remember making? Don't overthink it, I just need it so I can make a little surprise for you.

If they don't answer try to bring them back to an answer for each, as we need them before the workshop. If they have issues with their setup or with using Claude Code help and guide them through it.

Once they've answered both, replace each "the person" in CLAUDE.md with their first name, and change nothing else in the file.

Whatever they named making, however small or odd, is the subject. Turn it into an absurd law of interface design, one it could have taught a designer: a rule no one has heard of, stated with total confidence, that a designer would laugh at in recognition.

Make `setup-check/law.html`: one screen that reads as one piece, where the law leads and is followed by a hello from Brian, who'll run the workshop. Give it some personality. In the hello Brian greets them by name, reacts to their law in a line and welcomes them to Build it like you at SXC Stockholm 2026. Let the subject inform the look (its era, colours, type and materials), so the page looks like where the law came from. The law should be unique, intentionally designed, and readable at a glance.

When all of it worked, reply only: "Everything is set up and works! You're ready for the workshop. See you there." The page says the rest.

If writing a file or publishing fails, help them get it working: say plainly which step failed and what it said, and walk them through what they can try, help and guide them through it.
